### Checkpoint 1: energy and bonds

4 per attempt. Always: a-break, a-favor, a-why.

- **a-break** H and Cl are pulled apart, along the arrow. Breaking a bond is: *Endothermic: energy goes into the system* Bond breaking is endothermic. The bonding electrons sit low in energy, so the surroundings must give the system energy to pull the atoms apart.
  - Exothermic: energy goes out of the system: Exothermic means energy out, and that is bond making. Pulling two bonded atoms apart needs energy put in.
  - Neither: no energy changes hands: Every bond holds its atoms together; that hold has to be overcome, so energy must be supplied.
- **a-why** Why does breaking a bond take energy in? *Bonded electrons sit lower than in free atoms* Two atoms bond because their shared pair sits lower in energy than in the separate atoms (the bottom of the curve). Pulling them apart lifts the electrons back up, and that energy has to come in from the surroundings.
  - The atoms are lower in energy once they are apart: If the separate atoms were lower in energy, bonds would fall apart by themselves. Atoms bond because the shared pair sits lower.
  - Breaking a bond releases the energy stored in it: The most common mix-up: breaking a bond never releases energy. Energy comes out only when a bond forms.
- **a-favor** A reaction with ΔG < 0, like this one, is: *Exergonic and thermodynamically favorable* ΔG < 0 means the products are lower in free energy than the reactants: exergonic, and thermodynamically favorable (K > 1).
  - Endergonic and not thermodynamically favorable: Endergonic means ΔG > 0. Here ΔG < 0: the products are lower in free energy.
  - Endergonic and thermodynamically favorable: Endergonic means ΔG > 0, and then the reaction is not thermodynamically favorable. Start from the sign of ΔG.
- **a-homo** This is homolytic cleavage of H–Cl, with two fish-hook arrows. It gives: *Two radicals, one electron each* Homolytic: the bond splits evenly, one electron to each atom (two fish-hook arrows). Each atom has an unpaired electron: two radicals.

  - A cation and a radical: Each atom gets one electron back, so neither ends up charged. A cation would mean one atom lost its electron.

  - Two fish-hook arrows: one electron to each atom: Two fish-hooks move one electron each: that is homolytic cleavage. Hetero means different: both electrons go to one atom.

- **a-647** For a reaction with ΔH° < 0, which must be true? (Klein 6.47) *The bonds broken are weaker than the bonds formed* Breaking the old bonds takes energy in; making the new ones gives energy out.   - Exergonic: not thermodynamically favorable: Exergonic and not favorable never go together: exergonic is ΔG < 0, which is favorable. And here the products sit higher.

The reaction passes through it; it cannot be isolated. An intermediate sits in a valley and lives for a short time.

  - K < 1: K < 1 means the reactants are favored at equilibrium: ΔG > 0, not thermodynamically favorable.
  - K = 0: K is never 0: some products always form. Favorable means more products than reactants at equilibrium.

  - Any ion with a positive charge: A positive ion has no pair to give. A nucleophile is electron-rich, usually with a lone pair or a pi bond.
