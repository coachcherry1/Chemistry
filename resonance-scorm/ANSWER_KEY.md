# Answer key — Resonance

Generated by `node tools/answer-key.js > ANSWER_KEY.md`. Do not edit by hand: change the data in
`src/js/` and regenerate.

- **Equivalent resonance only.** Every species here gets the same structure from every choice Rule 5 can
  make, just turned around (ozone, nitrite, nitrate, carbonate, SO₂, SO₃, formate). Species whose choices
  give *different* structures — CO₂, HCO₃⁻, HNO₃, N₃⁻, SCN⁻, acetone — are left out (`tools/validate.js`
  rejects them).
- **The hybrid** is drawn with a solid line plus a dashed line for each partial double bond, a shaded cloud
  over the bonds that share the electrons, and δ− / δ+ where the average formal charge is negative /
  positive. Lone pairs are left out of the hybrid. Formal-charge numbers are never shown to students.
- **Bond lengths** are shown only for O₃, NO₂⁻, NO₃⁻ and CO₃²⁻. The point is qualitative: all the bonds are
  the same length, between a single and a double bond. (The real values sit nearer the double bond than a
  straight-line "average" would suggest; the activity never claims otherwise.) The sulfur species are left
  out on purpose: measured S–O bonds are as short as S=O, which is the expanded-octet picture this class
  does not use.
- **The 5 rules are unchanged** from the Lewis structures activity, including the anti-AI message for
  extra double bonds on S.

## What the students do at each step (none of it graded)

- **Build:** the 5-rule builder from the Lewis structures activity, unchanged.
- **Find every resonance structure:** they start from the structure after Rule 4 with structure A already
  found, click a lone pair on a different O, and add the new structure. Adding one they already have, or
  saying "I’ve found them all" too early, gets a hint. A second double bond on S gets the anti-AI message.
- **Hybrid:** predict the bond order (multiple choice), press *Average them* (the structures are laid on top
  of each other, then fade into the hybrid), then predict the bond length on a ruler and lock it in to see
  the measured value.

## Checkpoint questions (graded)

13 questions per attempt; the **first answer** to each counts. Each checkpoint always asks its
core questions (★) and fills the rest at random from its pool. Choices are shuffled on screen; here the
correct one is listed first. 🤖 marks a choice that triggers the anti-AI message.

### Checkpoint 1 — ozone and the idea of resonance — 3 of 5

**★ Which statement about ozone, O₃, is true?** `a-flip`

- ✅ Every ozone molecule is the hybrid: its two O–O bonds are identical, between a single and a double bond
- Each ozone molecule flips back and forth between its two resonance structures
- Half of all ozone molecules are structure A and the other half are structure B
- One O–O bond is a double bond and the other is a single bond

*Why:* Resonance structures are not real molecules taking turns. Ozone is ONE thing all the time — the hybrid. A mule is not a horse half the time and a donkey the other half; it is always a mule.

**★ What is different between ozone’s two resonance structures?** `a-what-moves`

- ✅ Only where the double bond and the lone pairs are — every atom stays in the same place
- Which atom is in the center
- How many valence electrons there are
- The two end O atoms swap places

*Why:* Resonance structures have the same atoms in the same places and the same number of electrons. Only electrons move: here, which end O shares its pair in Rule 5.

**What is the bond order of each O–O bond in the ozone hybrid?** `a-o3-order`

- ✅ 1½
- 1
- 2
- 1⅓

*Why:* Each O–O bond is double in one structure and single in the other: (2 + 1) ÷ 2 = 1½.

**Both O–O bonds in ozone are 128 pm long. A typical O–O single bond is 148 pm and an O=O double bond is 121 pm. What does that show?** `a-o3-length`

- ✅ Both bonds are the same: in between a single and a double bond — the hybrid
- Ozone has one single bond and one double bond
- Both of ozone’s bonds are double bonds
- Both of ozone’s bonds are single bonds

*Why:* If ozone really had one single and one double bond, one bond would be about 148 pm and the other about 121 pm. Instead both are 128 pm — between the two — which is exactly what the hybrid predicts.

**How can you tell, from the 5 rules, that a molecule or ion has resonance structures?** `a-when`

- ✅ Rule 5 has a choice: the double bond could go to more than one identical atom
- It has a double bond anywhere
- It has oxygen in it
- Its central atom has a lone pair

*Why:* Resonance shows up when Rule 5 could have gone more than one equally good way. CH₂O has a double bond, but only one O to put it on — so it has no resonance.

### Checkpoint 2 — nitrite and nitrate — 3 of 6

**★ Which drawing shows the resonance hybrid of the nitrate ion, NO₃⁻?** `b-no3-hybrid`

- ✅ [drawing of NO₃⁻] the hybrid: every shared bond a solid + dashed line, cloud, δ signs
- [drawing of NO₃⁻] one resonance structure (one double bond, the rest single)
- [drawing of NO₃⁻] every shared bond a full double bond
- [drawing of NO₃⁻] only some bonds drawn as partial double bonds

*Why:* In the hybrid all three N–O bonds are identical: each is a solid line plus a dashed line (a partial double bond), and the extra electrons are spread over all three O atoms (δ−). One double bond and two single bonds is just one resonance structure, not the real ion.

**★ How many resonance structures does the nitrate ion, NO₃⁻, have?** `b-no3-count`

- ✅ 3
- 1
- 2
- 4

*Why:* After Rule 4, N has only 6 electrons, so Rule 5 needs one O to share a pair — and any of the three O atoms will do. Three choices, three resonance structures.

**What is the bond order of each N–O bond in the nitrate hybrid?** `b-no3-order`

- ✅ 1⅓
- 1½
- 2
- 1

*Why:* Each N–O bond is double in one structure and single in the other two: (2 + 1 + 1) ÷ 3 = 1⅓.

**What is the bond order of each N–O bond in the nitrite hybrid, NO₂⁻?** `b-no2-order`

- ✅ 1½
- 1⅓
- 2
- 1

*Why:* Two resonance structures; each N–O bond is double in one and single in the other: (2 + 1) ÷ 2 = 1½.

**Where is the 1− charge of the nitrite ion in the hybrid?** `b-no2-charge`

- ✅ Spread over both O atoms — each carries part of it (δ−)
- On the single-bonded O atom only
- On the N atom
- It jumps from one O to the other

*Why:* In each resonance structure the single-bonded O carries the extra electrons — but it is a different O each time. In the real ion the charge is shared: each O is partly negative (δ−).

**Put these from LONGEST to SHORTEST: a typical N–O single bond, a typical N=O double bond, and an N–O bond in the nitrate ion.** `b-rank`

- ✅ N–O single, then nitrate’s N–O, then N=O double
- N=O double, then nitrate’s N–O, then N–O single
- Nitrate’s N–O, then N–O single, then N=O double
- N–O single, then N=O double, then nitrate’s N–O

*Why:* A higher bond order means a shorter bond. Nitrate’s bonds (bond order 1⅓) fall between a single bond (1) and a double bond (2): about 140 pm, 125 pm and 120 pm.

### Checkpoint 3 — carbonate — 3 of 6

**★ Which drawing shows the resonance hybrid of the carbonate ion, CO₃²⁻?** `c-co3-hybrid`

- ✅ [drawing of CO₃²⁻] the hybrid: every shared bond a solid + dashed line, cloud, δ signs
- [drawing of CO₃²⁻] one resonance structure (one double bond, the rest single)
- [drawing of CO₃²⁻] every shared bond a single bond
- [drawing of CO₃²⁻] only some bonds drawn as partial double bonds

*Why:* All three C–O bonds in carbonate are identical partial double bonds (bond order 1⅓), and the 2− charge is spread over all three O atoms (δ−).

**★ What are the three C–O bonds in the carbonate ion like?** `c-co3-length`

- ✅ All the same length — between a C–O single bond and a C=O double bond
- One short (double) and two long (single)
- All the same length as a C=O double bond
- All the same length as a C–O single bond

*Why:* All three are 129 pm: shorter than a C–O single bond (143 pm), longer than a C=O double bond (120 pm). Only the hybrid explains three identical bonds.

**How many resonance structures does the carbonate ion have?** `c-co3-count`

- ✅ 3
- 2
- 1
- 4

*Why:* C is 2 electrons short after Rule 4, and any of the three O atoms can share a pair: three structures.

**How is carbonate’s 2− charge spread out in the hybrid?** `c-co3-charge`

- ✅ Over all three O atoms — each is partly negative (δ−)
- On the two single-bonded O atoms only
- On the C atom
- On one O atom

*Why:* Which two O atoms are single-bonded changes from structure to structure. In the real ion every O carries part of the charge.

**What is the bond order of each C–O bond in carbonate?** `c-co3-order`

- ✅ 1⅓
- 1½
- 2
- 1

*Why:* (2 + 1 + 1) ÷ 3 = 1⅓ — the same as nitrate, which has the same shape and the same pattern of bonds.

**Which bond is SHORTER: a C–O bond in carbonate (bond order 1⅓) or the C=O bond in formaldehyde, CH₂O (a true double bond)?** `c-vs-ch2o`

- ✅ The C=O in formaldehyde — a higher bond order means a shorter bond
- The C–O in carbonate — it has more O atoms
- They are the same length
- The C–O in carbonate — because it has a charge

*Why:* Bond order 2 beats bond order 1⅓: the double bond is shorter (120 pm against 129 pm).

### Checkpoint 4 — putting it together — 4 of 7

**★ Which of these has resonance structures (by our 5 rules)?** `d-which`

- ✅ SO₃
- SO₄²⁻
- ClO₃⁻
- CH₂O

*Why:* SO₃: after Rule 4, S has only 6 electrons, and any of the three O atoms can share a pair. SO₄²⁻ and ClO₃⁻ never get to Rule 5 — their central atoms already have 8 — and CH₂O has only one O to share.

**★ Which is a correct resonance structure of SO₂ by the 5 rules?** `d-so2-rules`

- ✅ [drawing] S in the center with 1 lone pair; double bond to O lower right of it, O with 2 lone pairs; single bond to O lower left of it, O with 3 lone pairs.
- [drawing] S in the center with 1 lone pair; double bond to O lower right of it, O with 2 lone pairs; double bond to O lower left of it, O with 2 lone pairs. 🤖
- [drawing] S in the center with 1 lone pair; single bond to O lower right of it, O with 3 lone pairs; single bond to O lower left of it, O with 3 lone pairs.
- [drawing] S in the center with no lone pairs; double bond to O lower right of it, O with 2 lone pairs; single bond to O lower left of it, O with 3 lone pairs.

*Why:* 6 + 2 × 6 = 18. After Rule 4, S has 6 electrons, so ONE O shares a pair: O=S–O with a lone pair on S. The other O could share instead — that is the second resonance structure. Two double bonds would give S more than 8, and Rule 5 never does that.

**By our 5 rules, how many resonance structures does SO₂ have?** `d-so2-count`

- ✅ 2
- 1
- 3
- 0

*Why:* S needs one pair, and either O can give it: two equivalent structures, just like ozone and nitrite.

**Why does formaldehyde, CH₂O, have NO resonance structures?** `d-ch2o-why`

- ✅ Only the O can share a pair in Rule 5, so the double bond can go just one place
- C is in period 2
- It has no charge
- It has hydrogen atoms in it

*Why:* H atoms have no lone pairs to share, so the double bond can only go to the O. One choice means one structure — no resonance.

**What is the bond order of each C–O bond in the formate ion, HCO₂⁻?** `d-formate-order`

- ✅ 1½
- 1⅓
- 2
- 1

*Why:* Two O atoms can share the pair: two resonance structures, so each C–O is (2 + 1) ÷ 2 = 1½. The C–H bond stays a plain single bond — it is the same in both structures.

**What is a resonance hybrid?** `d-hybrid-def`

- ✅ The one real structure: an average of all the resonance structures
- The resonance structure with the most double bonds
- A mixture — some molecules are one structure and some are another
- The first structure the 5 rules give you

*Why:* The resonance structures are drawings we need because one Lewis drawing can’t show a partial double bond. The hybrid — their average — is what the molecule or ion really is.

**Which drawing shows the resonance hybrid of sulfur trioxide, SO₃?** `d-so3-hybrid`

- ✅ [drawing of SO₃] the hybrid: every shared bond a solid + dashed line, cloud, δ signs
- [drawing of SO₃] one resonance structure (one double bond, the rest single)
- [drawing of SO₃] every shared bond a full double bond 🤖
- [drawing of SO₃] only some bonds drawn as partial double bonds

*Why:* Three identical S–O bonds, each 1⅓, with the O atoms partly negative — the same pattern as nitrate and carbonate. Three S=O double bonds would mean Rule 5 ran three times, but S was only one pair short.

## The anti-AI messages

Shown when a student tries to give S a second double bond (SO₂ or SO₃ in Part 4, while finding resonance
structures or building) or picks a 🤖 choice in a checkpoint. A period-2 atom (C, N, O) is told it can never
have more than 8 instead. Nothing about it is sent to the LMS, and the count resets when the page reloads.
Edit `src/js/snark.js` to change the wording.

**First time, in a checkpoint:**

- Nice try, chatbot. Rule 5 stops the moment the central atom has 8 — one double bond, not a pile of them. Put the AI away and follow the 5 simple rules your teacher taught you.
- Rule 5 fixes a central atom that is SHORT, and stops when it has 8. Extra double bonds after that are the AI answer. Put the AI down and follow the 5 simple rules your teacher taught you.
