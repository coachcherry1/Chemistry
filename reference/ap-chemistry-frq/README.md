# AP Chemistry free-response questions (reference)

The College Board's released free-response questions (FRQs) and scoring guidelines for AP Chemistry, 2015 to 2026, kept here so each AP
activity can be checked against how the exam really asks about its topic. **These are not activities.** Nothing in this folder is built,
validated or uploaded to Schoology.

## Where acid-base and titration appear (a guide, not a substitute for reading them)

| Exam | Question | What it asks about |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2015 | 3 | A weak base (the sorbate ion) titrated with HCl: the net ionic equation, the concentration, the best indicator from a table of pKa values, the pH at the half-equivalence point, a sketch of the whole curve with the half-equivalence point marked, and which of the acid and its conjugate base predominates at a stated pH |
| 2016 | 2 (f) | A buffer of acetic acid and acetate: why the pH stays at 4.7 when HNO₃ is added, and the net ionic equation for it |
| 2016 | 4 | Phenol, a weak acid: the pH of a solution, then which pH values leave more than half of the phenol deprotonated (the pH against the pKa) |
| 2016 | 7 | Titration technique: reading the buret, the concentration of an unknown acid, and the effect of overshooting the end point |
| 2017 | 3 | HNO₂, a weak acid: making a buffer at a pH equal to its pKa, and the net ionic equation when acid is added |
| 2018 | 2 | A titration curve of HNO₂ with KOH: estimating the pKa from the curve, and which species is more concentrated after 15 mL of base |
| 2018 | 5 | HF in a 0.0350 M solution (13.0 percent ionized): which of two particle diagrams of the ionization is more accurate and why, Ka from the percent ionization, and whether dilution changes the percent ionization |
| 2019 | 3 | Carbonate as a base (Kb), and whether a carbonate solution can make a CO₃²⁻/HCO₃⁻ buffer at pH 6 |
| 2021 | 1 | Methanoic acid: the Ka expression and the pH of a 0.25 M solution, a Lewis diagram, then equal amounts of the weak acid and a weak base mixed (net ionic equation, and whether the result is acidic, basic or neutral) |
| 2022 | 1 | A titration curve of a weak acid with NaOH: estimating the pKa, and whether the weak acid or its conjugate base is higher at pH 4.00 |
| 2023 | 4 | Making a buffer from a weak base and its salt; the pH of the buffer; how diluting both changes it |
| 2023 | 5 | HCl as a gas, then as an acid: water around the Cl⁻ ion, and a particle diagram of one acid's ionization matched to a table of Ka values (HNO₂, HCl, HClO₄) with a justification |
| 2024 | 1 | Titration of lactic acid with NaOH: the pKa from the curve; a particle diagram of the flask matched to a point on the curve, with a justification; how the curve changes with a more concentrated base; calorimetry of the reaction |
| 2024 | 2 | Maleic acid and sodium bicarbonate; the ratio of species in a buffer at a stated pH |
| 2025 | 2 | Ascorbic acid: the concentration and the pKa from a titration curve; the ratio of conjugate base to acid at a stated pH |
| 2025 | 7 | The glycolate ion as a base; the Ka of glycolic acid |
| 2026 | 3 | Titration of nitrous acid: the pH at the equivalence point from the curve; the concentration; marking the point where the acid and its conjugate base are equal; the neutralization constant from Ka and Kw; whether methyl orange is a good indicator |

Titrations that are **not** acid-base also appear (redox: 2017 Q7, 2018 Q3, 2019 Q7, 2022 Q6) and are not listed above.

Patterns that matter when building an activity (see also `ap-chemistry/acids-bases/titration-curves-scorm/README.md`):

- Reading the **pKa from a titration curve** (half-equivalence point) appears in 2018, 2022, 2024 and 2025, and the concentration from the
  equivalence point in 2015, 2025 and 2026.
- **Which species is more concentrated** (the weak acid or its conjugate base) at a point or a pH comes up again and again: 2015, 2018, 2022, 2025.
- **Choosing an indicator** from a table: 2015 and 2026. The exam also titrates a **weak base with a strong acid** (2015 Question 3), which the
  titration activity does not cover yet.
- **Particle diagrams** tied to acid strength or to a point on a curve: 2018 Question 5 (which diagram of a weak acid is more accurate), 2023 Question 5 and
  2024 Question 1.
- Most parts are worth one point and want a justification, and many ask students to calculate.
