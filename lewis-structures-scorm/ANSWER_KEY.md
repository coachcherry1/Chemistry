## Also accepted

Rule 5 takes a lone pair from *any* outer atom, so in a few species more than one finished structure has
every octet. The builder accepts all of them (the tables above show the usual drawing):

- **HNO₂** — also: double bond to an O–H oxygen, single bond to O
- **H₂CO₃** — also: double bond to an O–H oxygen, single bond to O, single bond to an O–H oxygen
- **CO₂** — also: single bond to O, triple bond to O

A period-2 central atom is never allowed past 8 (the builder says so); a period-3-or-below central atom
pushed past 8 gets the anti-AI message.

*Why:* N is in group 15 (5 valence electrons) and F is in group 17 (7 each). 5 + 3 × 7 = 26. (12 forgets that there are three F atoms.)

**Which atom goes in the center of OF₂?** `a-of2-center`

- ✅ O
- F
- Either one — it doesn’t matter
- There is no central atom

*Why:* Rule 2: the central atom is the least electronegative one — the first written, not counting H. That is O. F is the most electronegative element of all and is never in the center.

**Why is H never the central atom?** `a-h-center`

- ✅ H can make only one bond — it is stable with a doublet (2 electrons)
- H is the most electronegative element
- H has no valence electrons
- H is always written first

*Why:* A central atom bonds to two or more atoms. H is full with 2 electrons, and one single bond already gives it that.

**CCl₄ has 32 valence electrons. After Rule 2 (C in the center, four single bonds), how many electrons are left to place?** `a-ccl4-left`

- ✅ 24
- 28
- 16
- 32

*Why:* Each single bond uses 2 electrons: 4 × 2 = 8, and 32 − 8 = 24. (28 counts only 1 electron per bond.)

**In the Lewis structure of NF₃, how many lone pairs are on N?** `a-nf3-lp`

- ✅ 1
- 0
- 2
- 3

*Why:* 26 − 6 (three bonds) = 20. Each F needs 3 lone pairs: 18 electrons. The last 2 go on N (Rule 4) as 1 lone pair, and N has 3 bonds + 1 lone pair = 8.

**★ After Rule 4 in CH₂O, C has only 6 electrons around it. What does Rule 5 tell you to do?** `a-ch2o-rule5`

- ✅ Move one of O’s lone pairs into the C–O bond, making a double bond
- Add one more lone pair to C
- Move a lone pair from an H into a C–H bond
- Nothing — 6 electrons is fine for C

*Why:* C is electron deficient by 2. Rule 5 shares one of O’s lone pairs as a second C–O bond: C=O. You can’t add a new lone pair — every electron was already placed — and H has no lone pairs to give.

**Which is the correct Lewis structure of HCN?** `a-hcn-struct`

- ✅ [drawing] C in the center with no lone pairs; triple bond to N right of it, N with 1 lone pair; single bond to H left of it.
- [drawing] C in the center with no lone pairs; single bond to N right of it, N with 3 lone pairs; single bond to H left of it.
- [drawing] C in the center with 1 lone pair; double bond to N right of it, N with 2 lone pairs; single bond to H left of it.
- [drawing] C in the center with 1 lone pair; triple bond to N right of it, N with 1 lone pair; single bond to H left of it.

*Why:* Rule 1: 1 + 4 + 5 = 10 electrons. After Rule 4, C has only 4, so Rule 5 runs twice: two of N’s lone pairs become bonds — a triple bond. That leaves N one lone pair and uses exactly 10 electrons. (H–C=N with a lone pair on C looks full, but it needs 12 electrons.)

## The anti-AI messages

Shown when a student tries to give a period-3-or-below central atom more than 8 electrons — in a build
(sharing a lone pair after the central atom already has 8) or by picking a 🤖 choice in a checkpoint.

to change the wording. `{X}` is the central atom, `{F}` the formula, `{n}` the count.

- Let me guess: the internet says {X} gets double bonds. The internet is not your chemistry teacher. Stop looking up answers with AI and use the 5 simple rules you were taught.
- Beep boop. This answer smells like AI. Nothing in the 5 rules your teacher taught you says to give {X} more than 8 electrons. Put the chatbot down and follow the rules.

- That’s the AI answer. Quit using AI to look up your chemistry answers and follow the 5 simple rules your teacher taught you.

- Again? Your AI isn’t enrolled in this class — you are. Quit looking up answers and follow the 5 simple rules.
