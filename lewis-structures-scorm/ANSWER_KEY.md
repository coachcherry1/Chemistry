# Answer key — Lewis structures, the 5 rules

Generated by `node tools/answer-key.js > ANSWER_KEY.md`. Do not edit by hand: change the data in
`src/js/` and regenerate.

Every structure below is what the 5 rules give, run exactly as written.

- **Double bonds come only from Rule 5**, and Rule 5 only runs when the central atom is electron deficient.
  A drawing with extra double bonds on a central atom that already has its electrons (S=O in sulfate, P=O
  in phosphoric acid) is treated as a looked-up answer — see *The anti-AI messages* at the end.
- **Incomplete octets:** B is stable with 6 and Be with 4, so Rule 5 leaves them alone (BF₃, BeF₂).
- **Expanded octets:** a central atom from period 3 or below keeps whatever Rules 2 and 4 give it, even more
  than 8 — SF₆ (six bonds from Rule 2), XeF₄ (leftover lone pairs from Rule 4). A period-2 atom never has
  more than 8.

To see any of these drawn, open `src/index.html?build=ID` (the IDs are in the last column of the full list).

## Also accepted

Rule 5 takes a lone pair from *any* outer atom, so in a few species more than one finished structure has
every octet. The builder accepts all of them (the tables above show the usual drawing):

- **HNO₂** — also: double bond to an O–H oxygen, single bond to O
- **H₂CO₃** — also: double bond to an O–H oxygen, single bond to O, single bond to an O–H oxygen
- **CO₂** — also: single bond to O, triple bond to O

A period-2 central atom is never allowed past 8 (the builder says so); a period-3-or-below central atom
pushed past 8 gets the anti-AI message.

*Why:* 6 + 3 − 1 = 8. Three O–H bonds use 6. The H atoms are already full (Rule 3 skips them), so the last 2 go on O: one lone pair.

**In Rule 3, why don’t the H atoms get any lone pairs?** `e-rule3-h`

- ✅ H is stable with a doublet, and its bond already gives it 2 electrons
- H has no electrons of its own
- H only gets lone pairs in Rule 4
- They do — every outer atom gets 3 lone pairs

*Why:* H is full with 2 electrons. One single bond is 2 shared electrons, so an H needs nothing more.

**How many lone pairs are on I in the periodate ion, IO₄⁻?** `e-io4-lp`

- ✅ 0
- 1
- 2
- 4

*Why:* 7 + 24 + 1 = 32 — the same count as perchlorate. Four bonds and 3 lone pairs on each O use all 32, so I gets no lone pairs and has exactly 8.

**S ends with 12 electrons in SF₆ but only 8 in SO₄²⁻. Why the difference?** `e-sf6-so4`

- ✅ In SF₆ Rule 2 gives S six bonds; in SO₄²⁻ four single bonds already give S its 8, so Rule 5 never runs
- F can make double bonds and O can’t
- SO₄²⁻ is an ion, and ions can’t have expanded octets
- There is no difference — SO₄²⁻ should have two S=O double bonds so S has 12 too 🤖

*Why:* An expanded octet only ever comes from Rules 2 and 4. SF₆ has six atoms to connect to S, so Rule 2 gives S six bonds. In sulfate, four single bonds already give S 8 electrons, so S is not electron deficient and Rule 5 never makes an S=O double bond.
