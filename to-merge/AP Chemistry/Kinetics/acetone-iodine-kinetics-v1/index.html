Kinetics of the Iodination of Acetone

Method of initial rates — vary [acetone], [H+] and [I2] to find the experimental rate law

Briefing — 1 of 6

The reaction

In acid solution, iodine reacts with acetone to give iodoacetone:

CH3COCH3  +  I2  —H+→  CH3COCH2I  +  HI

H+ is a catalyst — it is not consumed. Whether it still shows up in the rate law is one of the things you are going to find out.

Briefing — 2 of 6

Why this reaction is so convenient to time

I2 is amber; everything else in the flask is colourless. So you can follow the reaction with nothing but your eyes and a stopwatch: start the clock when the iodine goes in, stop it the instant the last of the colour disappears.

A colorimeter sits on the bench alongside you, logging the absorbance at 460 nm as each run proceeds. Beer's law says absorbance is proportional to concentration (A = εbc), so that trace is a direct picture of the iodine disappearing — and it should hit zero at the same moment your eyes say the colour has gone. Watch the shape of it. You will be asked about it later.

Briefing — 3 of 6

The method of initial rates

The rate law has the form

rate = k [CH3COCH3]m [H+]p [I2]n

You cannot reason out m, p and n from the balanced equation — orders are experimental. So you run the reaction several times, changing exactly one concentration at a time and holding the other two fixed. If a concentration doubles and the rate doubles, that order is 1. If the rate quadruples, it is 2. If the rate does not budge, it is 0.

Briefing — 4 of 6

What you will do

  1. Run 1 — a baseline mixture, volumes given to you.
  2. Runs 2, 3 and 4 — you design each one to change a single reagent. The bench will not let you run a mixture that changes two things at once.
  3. Analysis — read each order off the data, check the iodine order a second way against the three integrated rate law plots, write the rate law, and calculate k.
  4. Run 5 — you are handed a new mixture. Predict how long it will take before you run it, then find out.
Briefing — 5 of 6

Reagents on the bench

  • 4.0 M acetone  •  1.0 M HCl  •  0.0050 M I2  •  distilled water
  • Every run is made up to exactly 20.00 mL total — water makes up the difference. That fixed total is what lets you compare runs, and it means the concentration in the flask is always diluted from the stock: [X] = Mstock × VX / 20.00 mL.
Briefing — 6 of 6

Keeping a data table

Each run has a data table underneath the bench. Every reading and every quantity you calculate from it has to be entered before you can move on. Volumes are accepted within ±0.05 mL, stopwatch times must be copied exactly as displayed, and calculated concentrations, rates and k values within 5%.

Completion & grading

  • Your rate constant is randomly assigned to you, so your data will not match anyone else's. The orders will — that is the chemistry.
  • Finish all five runs and the analysis and a completion grade is submitted to Schoology automatically. There is no progress to save, so work straight through in one sitting.

Each integrated rate law predicts that a different function of concentration comes out as a straight line against time: zero order is straight for A, first order for ln A, second order for 1/A. Before you start

A quick reminder of how the lab runs:

  • Run 1 is given to you. For Runs 2, 3 and 4 you design the mixture — change one reagent at a time and let water bring the total to 20.00 mL.
  • Under every run is a data table. Each reading you take and each value you calculate must be typed in and checked. You cannot move to the next step until the current table is complete.
  • There is no way to save and return later. Give yourself enough time to work straight through all five runs and the analysis in one sitting.

Filling the data table out carefully as you go is what makes the analysis work — every order and every k value at the end is calculated from the numbers you entered.
