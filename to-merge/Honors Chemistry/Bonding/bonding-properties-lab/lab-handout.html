Objective

You will test the electrical conductivity of four substances as solids, liquids, and dissolved in water, and measure each one's melting point. Using your data, you will figure out how the way a substance is bonded (ionic or covalent) predicts both of these properties.

Background

Conductivity: Electric current only flows when charged particles are free to move around. A substance can only conduct electricity if two things are both true: (1) charged particles (ions) are present, and (2) those particles are free to move. Whether both conditions are met can change depending on whether the substance is a solid, a liquid, or dissolved in water.

Melting point: The substances you will test are held together in two different ways — as an ionic lattice, or as a covalent (molecular) solid. The strength of the attractions holding the solid together determines how much energy (and therefore how high a temperature) it takes to melt it.
