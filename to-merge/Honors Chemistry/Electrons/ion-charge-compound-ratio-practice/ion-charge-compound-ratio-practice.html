Ion Charge & Compound Ratio Practice

Correctly answer 10 problems to complete this activity. For each problem, two elements will be highlighted on the periodic table — one from a metal (cation) group and one from a nonmetal (anion) group. Based only on each element's position on the table, you'll identify the charge each ion forms, then figure out the ratio of cations to anions needed so the compound they form has an overall charge of zero. You get two attempts per problem — if you're still incorrect after your second attempt, you'll be walked through it step by step and it won't count toward your 10 completed problems. Your score starts at 100% and drops 4 points for every missed attempt, down to a floor of 60%. Your progress is saved if you're interrupted, and both a completion grade and a score are submitted to Schoology automatically when you finish.

Instructions:

  1. One cation-forming element (gold highlight) and one anion-forming element (blue highlight) are shown on the periodic table — only elements from groups 1, 2, 13, 15, 16, and 17 are used (hydrogen is excluded)
  2. Select the charge each ion forms, then select how many of each ion are needed so the compound's total charge is zero
  3. Click "Check Answer" once all four are selected
  4. Correct answers turn green; incorrect answers are marked with a red X — you get a second attempt
  5. Still incorrect after two attempts? You'll be walked through the problem step by step, and it won't count toward your 10 completed problems
  6. Complete 10 problems (within two attempts each) to finish the activity
  7. Before you close your Chromebook or exit Schoology, click the green "Save Progress" button so your work is saved and you can pick up where you left off next time.

Tip: Metals (groups 1, 2, 13) lose electrons to form positive cations. Nonmetals (groups 15, 16, 17) gain electrons to form negative anions. In any ionic compound, the positive and negative charges must add up to zero.
