# Net Ionic Equations: answer key

Generated by `node tools/answer-key.js` from the model in `src/js/net.js` (do not edit by hand). SCHEMA 1, about 45 minutes.

Every student gets a different set of problems and questions from a random seed; retakes get new ones. To see any
student's set, open `src/index.html?step=set&seed=<n>&problem=<1 to 8>&done` (or `&rung=<1 to 5>`), and
`?step=quiz&seed=<n>` for the questions. The seed is kept in the student's saved state.

## Scope

- Reactants: soluble salts (by the class solubility rules, the same data as package 3), the strong acids HCl, HBr, HI, HNO3, H2SO4 and the strong bases LiOH, NaOH, KOH, Ba(OH)2. No weak acids or bases, no gases, no solid reactants.
- Products: a solid, water (written HOH), or nothing (no reaction). Ammonium, acetate and sulfide are not used; an acid never meets a carbonate or a phosphate.
- Every problem gives the unbalanced molecular equation. Five steps, each graded on the first Check: balance, states, complete ionic, spectators, net ionic.
- Graded: 4 questions and 8 problems (3 that make a solid, 2 that make water, one of which must be reduced to lowest terms, 2 with no reaction, and last the challenge) = 44 items.
- Pools: 540 pairs that make a solid, 17 acid and base pairs, 1128 pairs with no reaction (lesson reactions excluded).

## The lesson (not graded)

**What (aq) looks like:** Na₂SO₄(aq), CaCl₂(aq), AgCl(s). The right picture shows the ions apart in the formula's ratio (a solid: on the bottom).

**Writing a substance as its ions** (the first is a worked example):

- Na₂SO₄(aq) → 2 Na⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq)
- MgCl₂(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + 2 Cl⁻(aq)
- 2 KNO₃(aq) → 2 K⁺(aq) + 2 NO₃⁻(aq)
- Al(NO₃)₃(aq) → Al³⁺(aq) + 3 NO₃⁻(aq)
- 2 K₃PO₄(aq) → 6 K⁺(aq) + 2 PO₄³⁻(aq)
- H₂SO₄(aq) → 2 H⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq)
- 2 BaSO₄(s) → stays together
- 2 HOH(l) → stays together

**Watch it happen** (a prediction, then the beaker):

- Silver nitrate + sodium chloride: Na⁺ and NO₃⁻. The spectators are in the solution before and after. The net ionic equation is what is left: Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s).
- Hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide: Water, HOH. Every strong acid with every strong base: H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → HOH(l). Na⁺ and Cl⁻ are spectators.
- Potassium chloride + sodium nitrate: Nothing happens: all four ions keep moving. If no solid and no water form, every ion is a spectator. Everything cancels: no reaction.

**Worked on the five steps:**

**Solutions of lead(II) nitrate and potassium iodide are mixed.** (a solid forms)

   - Given: Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) + KI(aq) → PbI₂ + KNO₃
   - Balanced, with states: Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2 KI(aq) → PbI₂(s) + 2 KNO₃(aq)
   - Complete ionic: Pb²⁺(aq) + 2 NO₃⁻(aq) + 2 K⁺(aq) + 2 I⁻(aq) → PbI₂(s) + 2 K⁺(aq) + 2 NO₃⁻(aq)
   - Spectators: NO₃⁻ and K⁺
   - Net ionic: **Pb²⁺(aq) + 2 I⁻(aq) → PbI₂(s)**

**Solutions of sulfuric acid and potassium hydroxide are mixed.** (water forms)

   - Given: H₂SO₄(aq) + KOH(aq) → K₂SO₄ + HOH
   - Balanced, with states: H₂SO₄(aq) + 2 KOH(aq) → K₂SO₄(aq) + 2 HOH(l)
   - Complete ionic: 2 H⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) + 2 K⁺(aq) + 2 OH⁻(aq) → 2 K⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) + 2 HOH(l)
   - Spectators: SO₄²⁻ and K⁺
   - Net ionic: **H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → HOH(l)**

**Solutions of copper(II) sulfate and sodium nitrate are mixed.** (no reaction)

   - Given: CuSO₄(aq) + NaNO₃(aq) → Cu(NO₃)₂ + Na₂SO₄
   - Balanced, with states: CuSO₄(aq) + 2 NaNO₃(aq) → Cu(NO₃)₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq)
   - Complete ionic: Cu²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) + 2 Na⁺(aq) + 2 NO₃⁻(aq) → Cu²⁺(aq) + 2 NO₃⁻(aq) + 2 Na⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq)
   - Spectators: Cu²⁺, SO₄²⁻, Na⁺ and NO₃⁻
   - Net ionic: **No reaction: every ion is a spectator.**

## Example questions (seed 1)

1. Which picture shows 2 Ca(NO₃)₂(aq), calcium nitrate dissolved in water? (Water molecules are not drawn.)
   - Answer: **Picture with the ions apart, 2 and 4**
   - Why: Ca(NO₃)₂(aq) is dissolved: its ions move apart. Each Ca(NO₃)₂ gives 1 Ca²⁺ and 2 NO₃⁻, so 2 of them give 2 and 4. Joined ions or a solid on the bottom would be (s).

2. Solutions of silver nitrate and iron(II) sulfate were mixed. This is the beaker afterwards. Which ions are the spectators?
   - Answer: **NO₃⁻ and Fe²⁺**
   - Why: NO₃⁻ and Fe²⁺ are still moving about in the solution, as they were before mixing: the spectators. Ag⁺ and SO₄²⁻ are in the solid, Ag₂SO₄.

3. Solutions of lithium phosphate, Li₃PO₄, and zinc bromide, ZnBr₂, are mixed. What is the net ionic equation?
   - Answer: **3 Zn²⁺(aq) + 2 PO₄³⁻(aq) → Zn₃(PO₄)₂(s)**
   - Why: Write the complete ionic equation, strike out the spectators (Li⁺ and Br⁻) and write what is left in lowest terms: 3 Zn²⁺(aq) + 2 PO₄³⁻(aq) → Zn₃(PO₄)₂(s)

4. Solutions of sodium hydroxide, NaOH, and hydrobromic acid, HBr, are mixed. What happens?
   - Answer: **Water, HOH, forms**
   - Why: Na⁺ and Br⁻ are the spectators. H⁺ and OH⁻ become water, HOH.
