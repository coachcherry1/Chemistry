# Learning review: net ionic equations

Reviews against the three checks in `CLAUDE.md` (Learning design), newest first. Not part of the zip.

## 2026-11-14: v2, after the teacher's first look

The teacher could not finish v1 and asked for these changes; each is made.

- **The complete ionic step was busywork** (counting ions and clicking charges again after the molecular equation was
  already balanced). Now the student only decides, for each substance, *splits into ions* or *stays together*; it is
  then written that way by itself, coefficients in front. The decision is the chemistry; the counting was not.
- **The net ionic step asked again what was already struck out.** Now it starts with what is left, coefficients first and
  with states; the student writes it in lowest whole numbers, or says no reaction when nothing is left.
- **Water drawn like an ion.** HOH is now the covalent molecule: O and two H at their van der Waals radii, bent at 104.5°.
- **Poured ions arrived in a clump.** They now enter spread across the surface, each moving its own way.
- **Too small.** Names sit beside the ions, never inside them; the beakers are about half again as large, the picture cards
  take the full width (the table is not needed there), the questions show their pictures two by two, and the text is larger.
- **"Strong electrolyte"** is not the class's term: it is gone from every student screen, and the validator bans it.
- **Ion charges:** the class knows them, so no table of charges is added (the open question from v1 is closed).

### 1. Write for a novice chemist

- Strong: the steps ask only for the new idea of each step (does it split; which ions are spectators; lowest terms).
- Watch: with the charges written for them, students no longer practise writing ions here. That is the teacher's choice
  (they know their charges); the ionic naming package is where it is practised.

### 2. Use the best cognitive science

- Strong: the effort is now on the chemistry, not the interface (desirable difficulty); each step still counts its first answer.
- Watch: the net ionic step is quick when nothing reduces (Check confirms it is already in lowest terms). It still catches
  the real slip (2 H⁺ + 2 OH⁻ → 2 HOH) and the no-reaction decision.

### 3. Dual-code everything

- Strong: the water molecule now looks like what it is; the split drill draws every substance in water after the answer,
  so the words and the picture meet on every card.

## 2026-11-14: v1, as first built

Prerequisites named in the plan: ion names and charges (the ionic naming package), balancing with the inventory
(packages 1 and 2), the solubility rules (package 3).

### 1. Write for a novice chemist

**Strong**

- One new idea per part: what dissolved means, a solid forms, water forms, nothing happens.
- Each term is defined in plain words where it first appears: "(aq)" in step 1, "spectator" in the step 3 instructions
  and the prediction feedback, "complete ionic" and "net ionic" in the rung that asks for them. All of them stay in reach
  in Big ideas at the end.
- The split drill opens with a worked example (Na₂SO₄, filled in and explained). The three lesson problems have a hint
  line on every step; the graded set has none.
- The five steps are named the same way everywhere and stay on screen as the strip.
- Feedback names the slip and the next move: the coefficient left out ("3 CaCl₂ gives 3 × 2 = 6 Cl⁻"), a wrong charge
  (with the ion's name), a solid or water split into ions, a non-spectator struck out (and what it became), a
  spectator kept, not in lowest terms (divide by what), the wrong state.

**Missing, with a proposed fix**

- **Ion charges are not on screen.** The solubility table shows the polyatomic ions with their charges, but not Al³⁺,
  Fe²⁺, Zn²⁺ and the like. A student who has not mastered charges fails the complete ionic step for that reason, not the
  new idea. Proposed fix (the teacher to decide): an "Ions" tab in the reference with the positive ions and their
  charges, in the lesson only, or everywhere.
- The word "electrolyte" is never used. Is the class term "strong electrolyte" wanted? It could go in step 1 and Big ideas.

### 2. Use the best cognitive science

**Strong**

- Generation: a prediction comes before every pour; Pour stays locked until it is made.
- Retrieval and calibration: the first answer to each of 44 items counts; the questions are not answerable from the screen
  above them.
- Interleaving: the graded set mixes solids, water, no reaction and the challenge, so the student must decide which it is.
- Spacing: every problem balances on the package 1 workbench and reads the package 3 solubility rules.
- Plan for error: every wrong choice is a known misconception (ions in a 1 : 1 ratio, ions still joined, a solid where
  nothing forms, the complete ionic equation offered as the net one, 2 H⁺ + 2 OH⁻ → 2 HOH).
- No opt out: after a miss the student keeps going until the step is right; Show me only after three misses.
- Desirable difficulty: the steps never reveal a no-reaction problem; no lesson reaction is graded.

**Missing, with a proposed fix**

- Elaboration: the "why" questions are only in the feedback. A short "why is K⁺ a spectator?" choice in a checkpoint
  question could be added if the teacher wants more.

### 3. Dual-code everything

**Strong**

- Every reaction, lesson and graded, is shown in the beaker beside its complete and net ionic equations, labels on the
  ions themselves, drawn to scale with a scale bar.
- Both directions: picture to words in the split drill (count the ions in the picture, write the split), words to
  picture in the picture cards and the first graded question (pictures as choices).
- Every question that names a mix draws it (the two solutions before mixing, or the beaker after).
- The pictures carry meaning: the counts are the formula's ratio, the solid has the formula's ratio, the spectators can be
  ringed, a new water molecule is ringed in green.

**Missing, with a proposed fix**

- The tiniest ions (Al³⁺, Mg²⁺, Cu²⁺) carry labels that are hard to read at their true size; tapping names them. If the
  teacher finds this a problem, the labels could sit beside the smallest ions instead of inside them.
