# Mole Conversions with Molarity: answer key

Generated by `node tools/answer-key.js` from the model in `src/js/`. Do not edit by hand. SCHEMA 2.

Every student gets their own substances and numbers (a seed saved with their progress), so this key gives the plan, the
rules, every substance and a worked sample set. Answers are computed by the activity from the model and checked by
`tools/validate.js` against a direct mole calculation and the formative quiz worked by hand.

## The class’s rules in the activity

- **Molar masses to the tenths place.** The on-screen periodic table shows every atomic mass rounded to tenths; the
  molar mass is those added up (CCl₄ = 12.0 + 4(35.5) = 154.0). Also accepted: the full masses added and rounded at the
  end (153.8), and the tenths value of a two-decimal table (Fe 55.85 gives 55.9, Cu 63.55 gives 63.6). The answer is
  then checked against the molar mass the student typed.
- **The roads of the mole map:** ___ g = 1 mol (the molar mass, a blank); 6.02 × 10²³ particles = 1 mol (Avogadro’s
  number as the class rounds it); 22.4 L of gas = 1 mol (gases at STP only); 1 mol compound = ___ mol element (the ratio
  from the formula, a blank); and 1 L solution = M mol, the molarity from the prompt, printed on its equality.
- **Molarity:** given in the prompt in mol/L, with the same significant figures as the volume (both are measurements). Two
  of the four molarity problems give the volume in mL, so 1000 mL = 1 L is one more step. Only substances that dissolve
  are used, at no more than half their solubility. L of solution and L of gas are different units.
- **Particles:** atoms of an element, molecules of a diatomic element or molecular compound. Ionic compounds are never
  counted in particles (their elements are: atoms O in Fe₂O₃).
- **Names:** every prompt names the substance and gives its formula ("9.340 g of sulfur dibromide, SBr₂"). The chain and
  the equalities use formulas.
- **Significant figures:** every equality counts as exact except the molarity; the given quantity decides. A right
  number with the wrong significant figures is not marked correct; the student is told the rule and tries again.
- **Tolerance:** answers and molar masses within 1.5% of the class’s count, for students using other periodic tables (below).
- **Scoring:** a graded problem counts when it is right before Show solution (Show solution asks to confirm). Hints and
  checking again are free. Score = problems right / 14, to `cmi.core.score.raw`; retakes keep the best.
- **Worked solutions** are typeset from LaTeX: each factor a fraction, cancelled units struck through.

## Other periodic tables: the 1.5% tolerance

A molar mass written to tenths, or an answer with the right significant figures, counts when it is within 1.5% of the
class’s value (an answer may also be within 1.5% of the value from the molar masses the student typed). A rounded answer
stands for everything that rounds to it, so a last digit that tips the other way still counts. Slips (a skipped subscript,
a miscounted H) are still named, even when they land within 1.5%. Every problem of 100 sets and their warm-ups was worked
with each table below: their own molar mass typed in the blank (to tenths) or the class’s, their own masses in the
calculator, the answer rounded to the right significant figures.

| Periodic table the student uses | Molar masses refused | Answers refused (own molar mass) | Answers refused (class molar mass) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Two decimals (H 1.01, C 12.01, Cl 35.45) | 0 of 1052 | 0 of 1900 | 0 of 1900 |
| Full masses (H 1.008, C 12.011, Cl 35.45) | 0 of 1052 | 0 of 1900 | 0 of 1900 |
| The class table but H 1.008 | 0 of 1052 | 0 of 1900 | 0 of 1900 |
| Whole numbers, Cl 35.5 (Mg 24, Fe 56) | 0 of 1052 | 0 of 1900 | 0 of 1900 |
| Whole numbers, Cl 35 | 0 of 1052 | 0 of 1900 | 0 of 1900 |

At 1% the two-decimal table (H 1.01 in grams of hydrogen) and the whole-number tables (Mg 24, Cl 35) were refused now and then;
the teacher chose 1.5%. `Chem.TOL` in `src/js/chem.js` is the one number to change, and `tools/validate.js` replays these tables.

## The formative quiz, worked the activity’s way

1. 21.55 L He gas to mol He (1 step)

$$21.55\ \text{L He gas} \times \frac{1\ \text{mol He}}{22.4\ \text{L He gas}} = 0.9621\ \text{mol He}$$

2. 53.0 g Mg(NO₃)₂ to mol Mg(NO₃)₂ (1 step)

$$53.0\ \text{g Mg(NO}_{3}\text{)}_{2} \times \frac{1\ \text{mol Mg(NO}_{3}\text{)}_{2}}{148.3\ \text{g Mg(NO}_{3}\text{)}_{2}} = 0.357\ \text{mol Mg(NO}_{3}\text{)}_{2}$$

3. 8.15 × 10²⁵ molecules PCl₃ to g PCl₃ (2 steps)

$$8.15 \times 10^{25}\ \text{molecules PCl}_{3} \times \frac{1\ \text{mol PCl}_{3}}{6.02 \times 10^{23}\ \text{molecules PCl}_{3}} \times \frac{137.5\ \text{g PCl}_{3}}{1\ \text{mol PCl}_{3}} = 1.86 \times 10^{4}\ \text{g PCl}_{3}$$

4. 9.340 g SBr₂ to atoms Br (3 steps)

$$9.340\ \text{g SBr}_{2} \times \frac{1\ \text{mol SBr}_{2}}{191.9\ \text{g SBr}_{2}} \times \frac{2\ \text{mol Br}}{1\ \text{mol SBr}_{2}} \times \frac{6.02 \times 10^{23}\ \text{atoms Br}}{1\ \text{mol Br}} = 5.860 \times 10^{22}\ \text{atoms Br}$$

## A sample graded set (seed 1)

1. Convert 0.591 L of 0.508 mol/L zinc nitrate solution, Zn(NO₃)₂, to mol Zn(NO₃)₂. (1-step, id `P01_Lsol_Zn_NO3_2_to_mol_Zn_NO3_2`) **0.300 mol Zn(NO₃)₂**

$$0.591\ \text{L Zn(NO}_{3}\text{)}_{2}\text{ solution} \times \frac{0.508\ \text{mol Zn(NO}_{3}\text{)}_{2}}{1\ \text{L Zn(NO}_{3}\text{)}_{2}\text{ solution}} = 0.300\ \text{mol Zn(NO}_{3}\text{)}_{2}$$

2. Convert 87.8 L of fluorine gas, F₂, at STP to atoms of fluorine, F. (3-step, id `P02_L_F2_to_part_F`) **4.72 × 10²⁴ atoms F**

$$87.8\ \text{L F}_{2}\text{ gas} \times \frac{1\ \text{mol F}_{2}}{22.4\ \text{L F}_{2}\text{ gas}} \times \frac{2\ \text{mol F}}{1\ \text{mol F}_{2}} \times \frac{6.02 \times 10^{23}\ \text{atoms F}}{1\ \text{mol F}} = 4.72 \times 10^{24}\ \text{atoms F}$$

3. Convert 0.07091 mol of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆, to atoms of oxygen, O. (2-step, id `P03_mol_C6H12O6_to_part_O`) **2.561 × 10²³ atoms O**

$$0.07091\ \text{mol C}_{6}\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_{6} \times \frac{6\ \text{mol O}}{1\ \text{mol C}_{6}\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_{6}} \times \frac{6.02 \times 10^{23}\ \text{atoms O}}{1\ \text{mol O}} = 2.561 \times 10^{23}\ \text{atoms O}$$

4. Convert 8.12 g of potassium sulfate, K₂SO₄, to g of sulfur, S. (3-step, id `P04_g_K2SO4_to_g_S`) **1.50 g S**

$$8.12\ \text{g K}_{2}\text{SO}_{4} \times \frac{1\ \text{mol K}_{2}\text{SO}_{4}}{174.3\ \text{g K}_{2}\text{SO}_{4}} \times \frac{1\ \text{mol S}}{1\ \text{mol K}_{2}\text{SO}_{4}} \times \frac{32.1\ \text{g S}}{1\ \text{mol S}} = 1.50\ \text{g S}$$

$$16\ \text{mL GaCl}_{3}\text{ solution} \times \frac{1\ \text{L GaCl}_{3}\text{ solution}}{1000\ \text{mL GaCl}_{3}\text{ solution}} \times \frac{0.080\ \text{mol GaCl}_{3}}{1\ \text{L GaCl}_{3}\text{ solution}} \times \frac{3\ \text{mol Cl}}{1\ \text{mol GaCl}_{3}} \times \frac{6.02 \times 10^{23}\ \text{atoms Cl}}{1\ \text{mol Cl}} = 2.3 \times 10^{21}\ \text{atoms Cl}$$
