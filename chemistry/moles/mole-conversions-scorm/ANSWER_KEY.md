# Mole Conversions: answer key

Generated by `node tools/answer-key.js` from the model in `src/js/`. Do not edit by hand. SCHEMA 2.

Every student gets their own substances and numbers (a seed saved with their progress), so this key gives the plan, the
rules, every substance and a worked sample set. Answers are computed by the activity from the model and checked by
`tools/validate.js` against a direct mole calculation and the formative quiz worked by hand.

## The class’s rules in the activity

- **Molar masses to the tenths place.** The on-screen periodic table shows every atomic mass rounded to tenths; the
  molar mass is those added up (CCl₄ = 12.0 + 4(35.5) = 154.0). Also accepted: the full masses added and rounded at the
  end (153.8), and the tenths value of a two-decimal table (Fe 55.85 gives 55.9, Cu 63.55 gives 63.6). The answer is
  then checked against the molar mass the student typed.
- **The roads of the mole map:** ___ g = 1 mol (the molar mass, a blank); 6.02 × 10²³ particles = 1 mol (Avogadro’s
  number as the class rounds it); 22.4 L of gas = 1 mol (gases at STP only); 1 mol compound = ___ mol element (the ratio
  from the formula, a blank).
- **Particles:** atoms of an element, molecules of a diatomic element or molecular compound. Ionic compounds are never
  counted in particles (their elements are: atoms O in Fe₂O₃).
- **Names:** every prompt names the substance and gives its formula ("9.340 g of sulfur dibromide, SBr₂"). The chain and
  the equalities use formulas.
- **Significant figures:** every equality counts as exact; the given quantity decides. A right
  number with the wrong significant figures is not marked correct; the student is told the rule and tries again.
- **Scoring:** a graded problem counts when it is right before Show solution (Show solution asks to confirm). Hints and
  checking again are free. Score = problems right / 12, to `cmi.core.score.raw`; retakes keep the best.
- **Worked solutions** are typeset from LaTeX: each factor a fraction, cancelled units struck through.

## Feedback on a wrong molar mass (example: Mg(NO₃)₂, 148.3)

| Typed | What it gets |
| --- | --- |
| 148.31 or 148 | Close: write it to the tenths place, with one digit after the decimal point. The class rounds every molar mass to tenths. |
| 86.3 | The 2 after the brackets multiplies every atom inside them. Mg(NO₃)₂ has 1 Mg, 2 N and 6 O. |
| 134.3 | The 2 after the brackets multiplies every atom inside them. Mg(NO₃)₂ has 1 Mg, 2 N and 6 O. |
| 54.3 | Count every atom: a subscript tells how many of the atom just before it. Mg(NO₃)₂ has 1 Mg, 2 N and 6 O. |
| 74.0 | Those look like atomic numbers. Use the atomic masses from the periodic table. |
| 124.0 | Did you leave out the Mg? Every atom in Mg(NO₃)₂ adds its mass: 1 Mg, 2 N and 6 O. |
| 120.3 | Did you leave out the N? Every atom in Mg(NO₃)₂ adds its mass: 1 Mg, 2 N and 6 O. |
| 52.3 | Did you leave out the O? Every atom in Mg(NO₃)₂ adds its mass: 1 Mg, 2 N and 6 O. |
| 172.6 | Count the Mg atoms again. Mg(NO₃)₂ has 1 Mg, 2 N and 6 O. |
| 162.3 | Count the N atoms again. Mg(NO₃)₂ has 1 Mg, 2 N and 6 O. |
| 164.3 | Count the O atoms again. Mg(NO₃)₂ has 1 Mg, 2 N and 6 O. |
| 132.3 | Count the O atoms again. Mg(NO₃)₂ has 1 Mg, 2 N and 6 O. |

Ratio blank, Mg(NO₃)₂ to O (6): 3 gets "The number after the brackets multiplies every atom inside them. Count the O atoms in Mg(NO₃)₂ again."; 2 gets "That is the number of N atoms in one Mg(NO₃)₂. This ratio is about O."; 9 gets "That counts every atom in Mg(NO₃)₂. Count only the O atoms."

## The formative quiz, worked the activity’s way

1. 21.55 L He gas to mol He (1 step)

$$21.55\ \text{L He gas} \times \frac{1\ \text{mol He}}{22.4\ \text{L He gas}} = 0.9621\ \text{mol He}$$

2. 53.0 g Mg(NO₃)₂ to mol Mg(NO₃)₂ (1 step)

$$53.0\ \text{g Mg(NO}_{3}\text{)}_{2} \times \frac{1\ \text{mol Mg(NO}_{3}\text{)}_{2}}{148.3\ \text{g Mg(NO}_{3}\text{)}_{2}} = 0.357\ \text{mol Mg(NO}_{3}\text{)}_{2}$$

3. 8.15 × 10²⁵ molecules PCl₃ to g PCl₃ (2 steps)

$$8.15 \times 10^{25}\ \text{molecules PCl}_{3} \times \frac{1\ \text{mol PCl}_{3}}{6.02 \times 10^{23}\ \text{molecules PCl}_{3}} \times \frac{137.5\ \text{g PCl}_{3}}{1\ \text{mol PCl}_{3}} = 1.86 \times 10^{4}\ \text{g PCl}_{3}$$

4. 9.340 g SBr₂ to atoms Br (3 steps)

$$9.340\ \text{g SBr}_{2} \times \frac{1\ \text{mol SBr}_{2}}{191.9\ \text{g SBr}_{2}} \times \frac{2\ \text{mol Br}}{1\ \text{mol SBr}_{2}} \times \frac{6.02 \times 10^{23}\ \text{atoms Br}}{1\ \text{mol Br}} = 5.860 \times 10^{22}\ \text{atoms Br}$$
