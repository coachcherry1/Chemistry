## Checkpoint: properties of liquids

8 questions per attempt from a pool of 19.

1. ★ `e-rule`: At the same temperature, liquid X has stronger IMFs than liquid Y. Compared with Y, liquid X has…
   - **Answer:** Higher surface tension, higher viscosity, and it evaporates more slowly
   - Others: Lower surface tension, higher viscosity, and it evaporates more slowly; Higher surface tension, lower viscosity, and it evaporates more slowly; Higher surface tension, higher viscosity, and it evaporates faster than Y
   - Why: Stronger IMFs hold the molecules together more tightly: the surface pulls in harder, the liquid resists flowing, and fewer molecules break free into the air.
2. `e-same-temp`: Why are the liquids always compared at the same temperature?
   - **Answer:** Warmer molecules move faster, which changes all three properties
   - Others: The IMFs only exist at room temperature, so a cold liquid has none; At other temperatures the molecules would have new Lewis structures; Surface tension can only be seen while the liquid is boiling hard
   - Why: Temperature is how fast the molecules move. To see what the IMFs do, everything else, temperature included, has to stay the same.
3. `e-evap-why`: Why does a liquid with weaker IMFs evaporate faster?
   - **Answer:** Its molecules break free of each other at the surface more easily
   - Others: Its molecules are heavier, so they drop out of the liquid sooner; The bonds inside its molecules break, so each molecule falls apart; Its molecules attract the air more strongly, and the air pulls them out
   - Why: Evaporation is molecules escaping from the surface. The weaker the attractions holding them back, the more of them get away.
4. `e-st-what`: What is surface tension?
   - **Answer:** How strongly the molecules at the surface are pulled in by their neighbours
   - Others: How quickly the molecules at the surface escape into the air above it; How thick the liquid is, and so how slowly it pours out of a pipet or a cup; How much heavier the surface of a liquid is than all the liquid underneath
   - Why: A molecule at the surface has neighbours only beside and below it, so the IMFs pull it inward. Stronger IMFs, a stronger pull: drops bead up.
5. `e-visc-what`: What is viscosity?
   - **Answer:** How strongly a liquid resists flowing, so how slowly it pours
   - Others: How quickly a liquid evaporates when it is left out in a dish; How strongly the surface of a liquid holds a drop in a tall bead; How many hydrogen bonds each molecule of the liquid can make
   - Why: A liquid with strong IMFs holds on to its neighbours as it moves past them, so it flows slowly: high viscosity.
6. ★ `e-decane-imf`: Which intermolecular forces act between decane, C₁₀H₂₂, molecules?
   - **Answer:** Only London dispersion forces
   - Others: Dipole-dipole forces and London dispersion forces; Hydrogen bonding, dipole-dipole forces and London dispersion forces; Dipole-dipole forces only, because it is such a long molecule
   - Why: Decane has only C–C and C–H bonds, so it is nonpolar and has nothing to donate. Only LDF, but with 82 electrons they are strong.
7. `e-diol-imf`: Which intermolecular forces act between 1,5-pentanediol molecules?
   - **Answer:** Hydrogen bonding, dipole-dipole forces and London dispersion forces
   - Others: Dipole-dipole forces and London dispersion forces; Only London dispersion forces; Hydrogen bonding and London dispersion forces, but no dipole-dipole forces
   - Why: Each end has an O–H: hydrogen bonding. The molecule is polar, so dipole-dipole forces, and like every molecule it has LDF.
8. `e-diol-pairs`: How many hydrogen bonds can one 1,5-pentanediol molecule make with its neighbours?
   - **Answer:** 2
   - Others: 1; 4; 6
   - Why: 2 H atoms on O and 4 lone pairs on O: the smaller number, 2, is how many hydrogen bonds it can make.
9. ★ `e-42-rank`: Pentane (42 electrons), 2-butanone (40) and 1-butanol (42). Rank them from strongest to weakest IMFs.
   - **Answer:** 1-butanol > 2-butanone > pentane
   - Others: 2-butanone > 1-butanol > pentane; pentane > 2-butanone > 1-butanol; 1-butanol > pentane > 2-butanone
   - Why: About the same electrons, so about the same LDF. 1-Butanol hydrogen bonds; 2-butanone is polar (its C=O) but has no H on O to donate: dipole-dipole forces; pentane has only LDF.
10. `e-alk-visc`: Which has the higher viscosity: pentane or decane?
   - **Answer:** Decane: 82 electrons to 42, so stronger LDF hold it together
   - Others: Pentane: its smaller molecules pack closer and hold on tighter; They are the same: both are hydrocarbons with only LDF between them; Pentane: it has fewer H atoms, so its molecules cannot slip past
   - Why: Both have only LDF. Decane has far more electrons, so it is more polarizable and its LDF are stronger: stronger IMFs, higher viscosity.
11. `e-alk-evap`: Which evaporates faster: pentane or decane?
   - **Answer:** Pentane: 42 electrons to 82, so weaker LDF let it break free
   - Others: Decane: its bigger molecules have more room to escape the liquid; They evaporate equally fast, because both have only LDF between them; Decane: it has more H atoms at its surface to escape into the air
   - Why: Fewer electrons, weaker LDF: pentane’s molecules break free of each other more easily, so it evaporates faster.
12. `e-alk-st`: Which has the higher surface tension: pentane or octane?
   - **Answer:** Octane: 66 electrons to 42, so its LDF pull harder
   - Others: Pentane: its smaller molecules sit closer at the surface; They are the same: both are nonpolar with only LDF; Pentane: it evaporates faster, so its surface is tighter
   - Why: Both have only LDF; octane has more electrons, so stronger LDF pull its surface molecules inward harder.
13. `e-42-visc`: Pentane and 1-butanol both have 42 electrons. Which has the higher viscosity?
   - **Answer:** 1-Butanol: its hydrogen bonds are stronger than pentane’s LDF
   - Others: Pentane: it has more C atoms, so its molecules tangle and catch; They are the same: they have the same number of electrons; Pentane: with no O atom, nothing lets its molecules slide past
   - Why: Same electrons, so about the same LDF, but 1-butanol also hydrogen bonds (its O–H). Stronger IMFs, higher viscosity.
14. `e-42-evap`: Pentane has 42 electrons and 2-butanone 40. Which evaporates faster?
   - **Answer:** Pentane: it has only LDF, while 2-butanone adds dipole-dipole forces
   - Others: 2-Butanone: it has 2 fewer electrons, so its LDF are a little weaker; They evaporate equally fast: their electron counts are almost the same; 2-Butanone: its O atom is heavy and pulls the molecule out of the liquid
   - Why: About the same electrons, so about the same LDF. 2-Butanone is polar, so it adds dipole-dipole forces: stronger IMFs, slower evaporation. Pentane evaporates faster.
15. `e-42-st`: Which has the higher surface tension: pentane or 2-butanone?
   - **Answer:** 2-Butanone: it is polar, so it adds dipole-dipole forces to its LDF
   - Others: Pentane: it has 2 more electrons, so its LDF pull its surface harder; They are the same: their electron counts are almost exactly the same; Pentane: nonpolar molecules always hold their surface the most tightly
   - Why: About the same electrons, so about the same LDF; 2-butanone is polar as well. Stronger IMFs, higher surface tension.
16. `e-58-visc`: 1-Hexanol and 1,5-pentanediol both have 58 electrons. Which has the higher viscosity?
   - **Answer:** 1,5-Pentanediol: 2 hydrogen bonds per molecule to 1-hexanol’s 1
   - Others: 1-Hexanol: it has one more C atom, so its chain is longer and stiffer; They are the same: both hydrogen bond and both have 58 electrons; 1-Hexanol: it has more H atoms, so it has more places to hold on
   - Why: Same electrons, both hydrogen bond, but 1,5-pentanediol has an O–H at each end: 2 hydrogen bonds per molecule to 1. Stronger IMFs, higher viscosity.
17. `e-58-st`: Which has the higher surface tension: 1-hexanol or 1,5-pentanediol?
   - **Answer:** 1,5-Pentanediol: it can make 2 hydrogen bonds per molecule, 1-hexanol 1
   - Others: 1-Hexanol: its longer chain of C atoms pulls the surface in more tightly; They are the same: both hydrogen bond, so their surfaces pull equally; 1-Hexanol: it has fewer O atoms, so its molecules sit closer together
   - Why: Both have 58 electrons and both hydrogen bond; the one with more hydrogen bonds per molecule has the stronger IMFs: higher surface tension.
18. `e-58-evap`: Heptane and 1-hexanol both have 58 electrons. Which evaporates faster?
   - **Answer:** Heptane: it has only LDF, while 1-hexanol also hydrogen bonds
   - Others: 1-Hexanol: its O atom makes it lighter, so it leaves the liquid first; They evaporate equally fast: they have the same number of electrons; 1-Hexanol: its O–H end sticks out of the surface and escapes first
   - Why: Same electrons, about the same LDF; 1-hexanol also hydrogen bonds. Weaker IMFs, faster evaporation: heptane.
19. `e-58-best`: Heptane, 1-hexanol and 1,5-pentanediol all have 58 electrons. Which has the highest surface tension?
   - **Answer:** 1,5-Pentanediol: the most hydrogen bonds per molecule
   - Others: 1-Hexanol: it has the longest chain of carbon atoms; Heptane: it has the most H atoms of the three liquids; All three are the same: they have the same electrons
   - Why: Heptane has only LDF; 1-hexanol hydrogen bonds once per molecule; 1,5-pentanediol twice. The strongest IMFs, the highest surface tension.
