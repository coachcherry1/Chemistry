## Predictions (never graded)

- **Find the lowest-energy conformation** — Br is a much bigger atom than C. In the lowest-energy conformation, which group on C2 will be anti to the CH₃ on C3? *Answer: CH₃.* The CH₃ goes anti, not the Br. A Br/CH₃ gauche interaction costs only about 1 kJ/mol — less than a third of a CH₃/CH₃ gauche interaction (3.8). The C–Br bond is 1.94 Å long, so the Br sits well away from its neighbours. Switch the 3D model to space-filling to see it.
- **Find the best staggered conformation** — How many CH₃/CH₃ gauche interactions will the lowest-energy staggered conformation have? *Answer: 2.* Two. With four CH₃ groups on two neighbouring carbons, some CH₃ has to be gauche to another. The best you can do is put the two H atoms anti, which leaves two gauche interactions (7.6 kJ/mol). The drawing showed the worst staggered conformation: three (11.4).
- **Swap the group on C2** — which group on the front carbon is anti to the back CH₃ in the lowest conformation? Cl: *CH₃* (1 kJ/mol); I: *CH₃* (1 kJ/mol); CH₃: *CH₃* (3.8 kJ/mol); CH(CH₃)₂: *CH(CH₃)₂* (3.8 kJ/mol); C(CH₃)₃: *C(CH₃)₃* (3.8 kJ/mol).
- **Put the bromines anti** — with the Br atoms anti, Molecule A's CH₃ groups are *anti* and Molecule B's are *gauche*. Replacing Br by I makes the halogen-gauche conformations *go up*.
- **Sketches** — students drag the predicted curve; the activity compares the number of peaks and the positions of the highest and lowest points with the real curve.

## Checkpoint questions (graded)

Each attempt draws 4 + 5 + 4 + 5 = 18 questions. Core questions (★) are always drawn; the rest of each checkpoint is filled at random from its pool. The first answer counts.

### Checkpoint 1 — ethane and the vocabulary — 4 of 9

1. **In a Newman projection, how is the FRONT carbon drawn?** ★ `a-front`
   - Answer: As the point where three bonds meet in the centre
   - Distractors: As the circle · As a wedge · It is not drawn at all
   - Explanation shown: You are looking straight down the C–C bond. The front carbon is the point where its three bonds meet. The back carbon hides behind it and is drawn as the circle, with its bonds starting at the edge.
2. **What does the dihedral angle in a Newman projection measure?** `a-dihedral`
   - Answer: The angle between a bond on the front carbon and a bond on the back carbon
   - Distractors: The H–C–H bond angle on one carbon (109.5°) · The angle between two bonds on the same carbon · How far the C–C bond tilts out of the page
   - Explanation shown: Looking down the bond, the dihedral angle is the angle you see between one front bond and one back bond. Turning the back carbon changes it; the bond angles on each carbon never change.
3. **What is this conformation of ethane called?** `a-eclipsed`
   - Figure: Newman projection of Ethane at 0° — eclipsed, 12 kJ/mol
   - Answer: Eclipsed
   - Distractors: Staggered · Anti · Gauche
   - Explanation shown: Every back C–H bond sits directly behind a front C–H bond (printed slightly offset so you can see it). That is eclipsed.
4. **What is this conformation of ethane called?** `a-staggered`
   - Figure: Newman projection of Ethane at 60° — staggered, 0 kJ/mol
   - Answer: Staggered
   - Distractors: Eclipsed · Totally eclipsed · Gauche
   - Explanation shown: Every back bond points exactly between two front bonds. That is staggered — the lowest-energy shape of ethane.
5. **Why is eclipsed ethane higher in energy than staggered ethane?** ★ `a-torsional`
   - Answer: Torsional strain: the C–H bonds on the two carbons line up, and the electrons in those bonds repel
   - Distractors: Steric strain: the hydrogen atoms are big enough to bump into each other · The C–C bond has to stretch to reach the eclipsed shape · Eclipsed ethane has fewer bonds

### Checkpoint 3 — halogens and branched groups — 4 of 9

1. **Looking down C2–C3 of 2-bromobutane, which conformation is lowest in energy?** ★ `c-best`

   - Distractors: A Br atom is smaller than a carbon atom · Br is attracted to the CH₃ group · Br/CH₃ is torsional strain, not steric strain
   - Explanation shown: A bigger atom, but on a longer bond. A CH₃ also carries three H atoms that poke toward its neighbours; a Br is a smooth ball held farther out.
